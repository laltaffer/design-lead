# encode — a correction becomes permanent state

The compounding step. A design correction given once and re-learned next session is
the failure this pack exists to end. `encode` fires when the human corrects a visual
choice, when a critique verdict deserves permanence, or when a project locks or
changes direction.

## Where each kind of fact lives

| The fact | Home |
|---|---|
| What a project shipped (world, palette, signature moves) | its row in `Design-Registry.md` |
| A move THIS model keeps reaching for across projects | the model-tells list in `Design-Registry.md` |
| A face a project shipped, or a face to avoid and why | `Typeface-Registry.md` |
| A rejection and its why ("too angular, hard to read") | the project's design notes, VERBATIM, plus a registry row update if it generalizes |
| The user's personal conventions and standing vetoes | `LOCAL.md` |

One home per fact. A fact recorded in two places will contradict itself within a
month.

## Writing a model-tell

A tell earns its row when a move appears in 2+ outputs across different worlds —
that's the model's hand showing, not the brief's. Record:

- the move, concretely ("numbered No. 1/2/3 index lines on any set of three things"),
- where it was caught (projects, date),
- the correction verbatim if the human gave one,
- **the replacement direction** — what stronger move takes its place.

The replacement line is mandatory. Tells are common because they work; the list is
LINT, not a brief, and a round of comps mechanically stripped of tells came back
unanimously worse. Encode what to reach for, not just what to drop.

## Writing a rejection

Capture the human's words verbatim, not a paraphrase — "the tools feel overwhelming"
sanded down to "user prefers simplicity" loses the information that made the
correction actionable. Add the why and the how-to-apply in your own words below the
quote.

## Discipline

- Before writing, check for an existing entry that already covers it — update it
  rather than duplicating; delete entries proven wrong.
- Never encode volatile facts (a current severity score, a screenshot's contents);
  encode the rule and the command that re-derives the state.
- Never encode a diagnosis before it's confirmed — a wrong explanation in a registry
  outlives the mistake.
- After writing, quote the new entry back to the human in one line so they can veto a
  bad generalization on the spot.

Done: the entry is in its one home with its why, the human saw it, and nothing else
changed.
